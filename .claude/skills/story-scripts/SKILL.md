# Story scripts for short-form

A short-form story is a promise kept late. Hook with a question, hold it open, pay it off at the end.

## Structure (Hook → Loop → Escalate → Twist → Payoff → Loop back)

| Beat | Time (of ~60 s) | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0–3 s | Ask the question or make the bold claim. One sentence. No intro, no "hey guys". |
| **Promise** | 3–5 s | Tell them they'll get the answer: "Here's exactly what would happen." |
| **Setup** | 5–20 s | Concrete, sensory specifics. Show the world in 2–3 vivid details. |
| **Escalate** | 20–40 s | "But here's the catch…" Raise the stakes. Every beat links with *but* or *so*, never *and then*. |
| **Twist** | 40–50 s | Reframe what we've seen. The emotional turn. |
| **Payoff** | 50–57 s | Close the loop the hook opened. |
| **Loop back** | last 3 s | A question to the viewer, or a line that flows into the first line for rewatches. |

- **Open loop ≠ hook.** The hook is the promise in the first 3 s; the loop is the tension that isn't resolved until the last 5 s. Never reveal the answer early.
- **Add a mini-payoff every 8–12 s** (a joke, a surprising detail, a new question) so the middle doesn't sag.
- **But / therefore:** if two beats connect with "and then", rewrite. Cause and conflict keep people watching.

## Writing for the ear

- Short sentences. One idea per line. Contractions always ("you'd", "it's").
- **Specific beats general:** "A bug at 3 AM" beats "technical problems".
- Use lists of three with rhythm: "Traffic. Loading bars. Microwave timers."
- Give the key word its own sentence or put it at the end of the line, where stress falls naturally.
- Leave room for silence: an ellipsis before a reveal, a one-word line ("Wow.") after a big moment.
- Read it aloud. Any line you stumble on, rewrite.
- Target ~150 words per minute of finished video (voice + pauses).

## Checklist

- [ ] First line works with sound off as on-screen text.
- [ ] The hook's question is only answered in the last 10 s.
- [ ] No beat longer than ~12 s without a new detail or turn.
- [ ] At least one "but" turn and one emotional twist.
- [ ] Ends on a question or a line that loops.
- [ ] Every line is sayable in one breath.
